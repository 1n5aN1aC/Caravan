# Caravan — Multiplayer Web Implementation



## Context



Caravan is the in-fiction card game from *Fallout: New Vegas*. It has no faithful,

playable multiplayer version on the web, and its rules are notoriously badly

explained — the wikis, guides, and the shipped game itself disagree on several

points. The goal is a self-hosted, browser-based, real-time 1v1 Caravan that is

**rules-correct first**: a trustworthy engine you can point at and say "that's how

it actually works," wrapped in the minimum UI needed to play it.



`C:\Users\joshua\Desktop\Caravan` is currently empty. This is greenfield.



v1 target: run the server on a machine, share a room code over LAN, two people

play a complete, correct match in their browsers. Nothing else.



**Deliberately out of scope for v1:** accounts, deck building, AI opponents,

matchmaking, ranking, chat/emotes, spectators, rematch, animation, persistence

across restarts, public hosting. Each is discussed under "Designed-for extensions."

## The Ruleset (authoritative — this is the spec)



Sources conflict; every conflict below was resolved as a deliberate decision.

Base ruleset is **FNV-faithful with known bugs excluded**.



### Setup

- Exactly 2 players. Each has **3 caravan tracks**, facing the opponent's 1-v-1, 2-v-2, 3-v-3.

- Each player gets their **own standard 54-card deck** (52 + 2 Jokers), shuffled with a seeded PRNG.

- Opening hand: **8 cards**. First player chosen at random.

- **Auto-mulligan:** if a dealt hand contains fewer than 3 number cards (A–10), that

  player's deck is silently reshuffled and redealt. Repeat until legal. Invisible to

  both players. (~34% of hands trigger this, so it will fire often.)



### Opening round

- 3 alternating turns each. Each turn places **one number card (A–10) onto one of your

  own empty caravans**.

- No face cards, no discarding, no disbanding, no drawing during the opening round.

  (This excludes the FNV opening-discard bug.)

- Hands settle at 5 after the opening.



### Turn actions (after opening) — choose exactly one

1. **Play a card** from hand → then draw 1 (if deck non-empty).

2. **Discard a card** from hand → then draw 1 (if deck non-empty).

3. **Disband** one of your own caravans that has ≥1 card → **no draw**.



Invariant: you draw exactly when you spent a card. Hand size is 5 while your deck holds cards.



### Number card placement

- Number cards go on **your own caravans only**. Ace = 1.

- Onto an **empty** caravan: any number card.

- **Equal rank is always illegal** — checked *before* direction/suit. A card whose rank

  equals the current top card's rank is never playable, regardless of suit or Queens.

- Onto a caravan with **1 card**: any number card of a different rank.

- Onto a caravan with **2+ cards**: must either

  - continue the caravan's **effective direction**, or

  - match the caravan's **effective suit** (this bypasses direction entirely).

- Values need not be adjacent — 3 → 7 is a valid ascending step.



### Derived caravan state (never stored — always computed)

- **Base direction** = comparison of the **two most recent number cards**. Recomputed

  after every change, so a legal off-direction same-suit play flips the direction, and

  a Jack removing a top card restores the prior direction automatically.

- **Effective direction** = base direction, flipped once per Queen attached to the

  **current top number card** (two Queens cancel).

- **Effective suit** = the top number card's printed suit, overridden by the most recent

  Queen attached to **that card**.

- Queens only ever govern plays onto the card they sit on. Once a new number card lands

  on top, the old card's Queens stop mattering. Queen state is local, not caravan-wide.

- **Value** = sum of number card values, each doubled once per King attached to it

  (Kings compound: 9 + 2 Kings = 36). Direction and suit comparisons use the card's

  **printed rank and suit** — Kings never affect them.



### Face cards

- Playable on **any number card on the table**, yours or your opponent's.

- **Max 3 face cards attached to one number card** (named config constant).

- Face cards cannot be played on an empty caravan or attached to another face card.



| Card | Effect |

|---|---|

| **Jack** | Destroys the number card it's played on, plus that card's attached face cards. Cards below remain; direction recomputes. |

| **Queen** | Reverses direction and overrides suit — scoped to the card she sits on (see above). |

| **King** | Doubles the current (already-modified) value of the card it sits on. Stacks. |

| **Joker on an Ace** | Destroys every other **number card of that suit** anywhere on the table (all 6 caravans, both players), along with their attached face cards. |

| **Joker on a 2–10** | Destroys every other **card of that rank** anywhere on the table, along with their attached face cards. |



Joker notes: the target card survives and the Joker stays attached to it. Hands and

decks are never touched. Board-wide reach is intentional — it's what makes Jokers matter.



### Selling and winning

- A caravan **sells** at **21–26 inclusive**.

- **Overburdened (>26):** the caravan stays on the table and remains fully playable —

  a Jack or Joker can bring it back under 26 and rescue it. It simply never sells.

  Clearing it is a **voluntary disband**, never automatic. (Rejects game8's "auto-discard".)

- A **track is decided** when at least one of its two caravans is sold AND strictly beats

  the other. An unsold caravan always loses to a sold one. Two sold caravans of **equal

  value are a tie** → track undecided.

- **The match ends only when all three tracks are simultaneously decided.** Whoever holds

  2+ wins. (With 3 tracks and no ties permitted at resolution, someone always has 2+.)

  This is why you can't sell two and coast — you must break the third.

- Evaluated after every move.



### Exhaustion and stalemate

- Empty deck → no draw; hand shrinks. Play continues.

- A player with **no legal action** (cannot play any card, has no cards to discard, and

  has no caravan to disband) **loses**.

- **Hard turn cap** (config constant, e.g. 300 plies) as a backstop against a pathological

  non-terminating match. Resolves as a draw.

## Architecture



**pnpm monorepo, TypeScript everywhere.**

packages/rules/     Pure rules engine. Zero I/O. No framework. Injected seeded PRNG.
packages/protocol/  Wire message types + Zod schemas. Shared by server and web.
apps/server/        Node + `ws`. Authoritative. Serves built client on the same origin.
apps/web/           React + Vite. Thin renderer + local legality highlighting.

`packages/rules` is the centerpiece and gets built and trusted **first**. It is imported

by the server as the authority, and by the client purely to compute legal move

highlighting without a round-trip. Rules logic is written and tested exactly once.



### Authority

Fully **server-authoritative**. The server owns deck order, both hands, and all state.

Clients send *intents* (`{play, cardId, target}`) and receive only what that seat may see.

Client-side rules are advisory UI only; the server independently re-validates every move

and a rejected intent triggers a snapshot resync.



### Transport

Plain WebSocket (`ws`) with a hand-rolled discriminated-union protocol (~12 message types)

validated by Zod at both ends. Shared TS types mean the compiler catches protocol drift.

Heartbeat + reconnect hand-written (~50 lines).



### Sync model

**Redacted full-state snapshot + event log.** Full state is tiny (6 caravans of ≤10 cards,

two 5-card hands), so every turn each player receives a complete snapshot with the

opponent's hand reduced to a count and both decks reduced to counts, plus the list of

events since the last snapshot (for the move log, and for animation later). Desync is

structurally impossible and reconnect resync is free.



**Redaction is a single choke-point function** — `redactFor(seat, state)` — and it is the

only path by which state reaches the wire. Nothing else may serialize a match.



### Match lifecycle

- **Store:** `Map<roomCode, Match>` in one Node process, behind a `MatchStore` interface

  so Redis is a one-file swap later. Server restart kills in-flight matches — accepted.

- **Rooms:** host creates → 4-letter code → second player joins → match begins. Third

  joiner rejected.

- **Seats:** on join, server issues an **HMAC-signed seat token** stored in `localStorage`.

  Reconnecting with it restores the seat and re-sends a full snapshot. Tab refresh is a

  non-event.

- **Disconnect:** opponent sees "Opponent reconnecting…" with a **90s countdown**. After

  90s the match ends as *abandoned* (no loss — there's no rating).

- **Turn timers:** none. State carries a nullable `turnDeadline` field so a timer is a

  config flip later.

- **Idle sweep:** rooms with no activity for **10 minutes** are garbage-collected.

- **Game over:** result screen → both players return to the landing page. No rematch;

  the room is destroyed.



### Determinism

The engine takes an **injected seeded PRNG** — it is the only source of randomness, and

`Math.random()` must never appear in `packages/rules`. Every match records its seed and

ordered move list, which makes any match deterministically replayable.

## UI (v1: minimal, unstyled)



Functional layout only — boxes, text, Unicode suit glyphs. No theming, no animation.

Purpose is to make the rules provable and playable as fast as possible.



- **Interaction:** click-to-select, then click a target. Selecting a card in hand asks the

  shared rules engine for **every legal destination**, and those targets highlight.

  Identical on desktop and touch, keyboard-navigable, and the highlighting is the single

  best teaching tool for Caravan's confusing placement rules.

- **Board:** opponent's 3 caravans across the top, yours across the bottom, paired by

  track. Each caravan shows its cards, attached face cards, current value, effective

  direction and suit, and a sold/overburdened badge.

- **Hand:** your 5 cards; opponent shown as a count. Deck counts shown for both.

- **Move log:** plain text list driven by the event stream.

- Style layer is kept in one place so a retro-terminal theme (amber/green phosphor,

  scanlines, monospace, CSS-drawn card faces) can be applied later without touching

  component structure.

## Correctness strategy



Caravan's bugs live in **rule interactions** (Queen + suit exception + Jack + recomputed

direction), not in individual functions. Three layers:



1. **Unit tests** (Vitest) per rule in `packages/rules`.

2. **Scenario fixtures** — a plain-text DSL where a file states a board, a move, and the

   expected result. Every edge case resolved above becomes one readable fixture file.

   Explicit fixtures required for: equal-rank rejection under a Queen-changed suit;

   direction flipping after an off-direction same-suit play; Jack on the top card

   restoring prior direction; Queen parity cancellation; King compounding; Joker-on-Ace

   vs Joker-on-number blast radius across all six caravans; the 3-face-card cap;

   overburdened caravan rescued back under 26; tie leaving a track undecided; match

   ending only when all three tracks decide.

3. **Property tests** asserting invariants: card conservation (every card is in exactly

   one of deck/hand/board/discard), hand ≤ 5, hand == 5 while deck non-empty, direction

   always derivable, value always recomputable from the board.



**Seeded match replay** is the debugging tool: seed + move list reproduces any match

exactly, so "it did something weird" converts directly into a new fixture.

## Build order



Each phase is independently verifiable.



1. **Rules engine core** — card/state types, immutable state, seeded PRNG, deal +

   auto-mulligan, opening round, number card legality (rank/direction/suit), derived

   direction/suit/value. Unit tests + first fixtures.

2. **Face cards** — Jack, Queen, King, Joker with full targeting rules and the 3-card cap.

   This is the hardest part of the engine; fixture coverage here is the priority.

3. **Win/loss resolution** — selling, ties, track decision, all-three-decided end

   condition, disband, exhaustion, no-legal-move loss, turn cap. Property tests.

   *Engine is now complete and trusted before any networking or UI exists.*

4. **Protocol + server** — `packages/protocol` message types and Zod schemas, `ws` server,

   `MatchStore`, room codes, seat tokens, `redactFor`, move validation, snapshot broadcast,

   reconnect grace, idle sweep, replay recording.

5. **Web client** — landing page (create/join), board rendering, click-to-select

   interaction with legal-target highlighting via the shared engine, move log, connection

   status, game-over screen.

6. **End-to-end play** — two browsers on LAN, full match. Fix what surfaces; every bug

   becomes a fixture.

## Verification



- `pnpm test` — unit, fixture, and property suites in `packages/rules` all green.

- Fixture coverage explicitly asserted for each edge case listed in "Correctness strategy".

- Server smoke: start server, create a room, join from a second client, play a scripted

  move list, assert the expected winner.

- Manual end-to-end: run the server, open two browser tabs (and a second machine on LAN),

  play a complete match to a decided result.

- Reconnect: mid-match, kill one tab's network / refresh it, confirm the seat restores

  from `localStorage` and the board is intact. Confirm the 90s expiry ends the match cleanly.

- Rejection path: send a hand-crafted illegal intent over the socket and confirm the

  server rejects it and resyncs rather than applying it.

- Redaction: inspect raw socket frames and confirm the opponent's hand contents and deck

  order never appear.

- Determinism: replay a recorded seed + move list and confirm an identical final state.

## Designed-for extensions (not built in v1)



The v1 structure deliberately accommodates these without rework:



- **Deck building** — deck construction is already abstracted behind a source interface;

  per-player custom decks (FNV: min 30 cards, no duplicate of the same card from the same

  set) drop in without touching game logic.

- **Retro-terminal theme** — isolated style layer.

- **Animation** — the event log already carries everything needed to animate.

- **Turn timers** — nullable `turnDeadline` field already in state.

- **Redis / multi-process** — `MatchStore` interface.

- **Rule variants** — ruleset constants (face-card cap, sell range, hand size, turn cap)

  are named config, not literals.

- **AI opponent, accounts, matchmaking, spectators, rematch** — additive; none require

  changing the engine or the authority model.

## Open risk



The rules resolutions above are reconstructed from sources that genuinely disagree, and

the two most authoritative (fallout.fandom.com and fallout.wiki) block automated access —

their content came via search snippets, not full pages. The largest remaining uncertainty

is **whether FNV recomputes direction from the top two cards or locks it on the first two**

(we chose recompute). If play testing suggests otherwise, that is a localized change to

one derived-state function plus its fixtures, precisely because direction is derived and

never stored.
